✨ The Path to Liberation ✨

As we delve into the fifth chapter of the Bhagavad Gita, we find ourselves in the midst of a profound discussion on the nature of the self and the path to liberation. Srila Prabhupada's translation and purports have made this sacred text accessible to us, and it is our privilege to delve into its wisdom.

🕉 The Transcendentalist's Journey 🕉

The chapter begins by describing the transcendentalist, who has shut out all external sense objects, keeping the eyes and vision concentrated between the two eyebrows, suspending the inward and outward breaths within the nostrils, and thus controlling the mind, senses, and intelligence. As the Gita says, "Shutting out all external sense objects, keeping the eyes and vision concentrated between the two eyebrows, suspending the inward and outward breaths within the nostrils, and thus controlling the mind, senses and intelligence, the transcendentalist aiming at liberation becomes free from *desire, fear and anger*" [Bg. 5.27-28](https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/5/27/).

This is the state of being where one is no longer controlled by the whims of the material world. The transcendentalist has transcended the bondage of desire, fear, and anger, and is thus free to pursue the path of liberation.

✨ The Happy Mystic ✨

The Gita further describes the perfect mystic, who is one whose happiness is within, who is active and rejoices within, and whose aim is inward. As the verse says, "One whose happiness is within, who is active and rejoices within, and whose aim is inward is actually the perfect mystic. He is liberated in the Supreme, and ultimately he attains the Supreme" [Bg. 5.24](https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/5/24/).

This is the state of being where one is no longer dependent on external sources of happiness. The perfect mystic finds joy and fulfillment within, and is thus free to pursue the path of self-realization.

✨ The Assured Liberation ✨

The chapter also assures us that those who are free from anger and material desires, self-realized, self-disciplined, and constantly endeavoring for perfection are assured of liberation in the Supreme in the very near future. As the Gita says, "Those who are free from anger and all material desires, who are self-realized, self-disciplined and constantly endeavoring for perfection, are assured of liberation in the Supreme in the very near future" [Bg. 5.26](https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/5/26/).

This is the state of being where one is fully surrendered to the will of the Supreme Lord ✨

Finally, the chapter concludes by describing the person who is in full consciousness of the Supreme Lord, knowing Him to be the ultimate beneficiary of all sacrifices and austerities. As the Gita says, "A person in full consciousness of Me, knowing Me to be the ultimate beneficiary of all sacrifices and austerities, the Supreme Lord of all planets and demigods, and the benefactor and well-wisher of all living entities, attains peace from the pangs of material miseries" [Bg. 5.29](https://vedabase.io/en/library/bg/5/29/).

This is the state of being where one has realized the ultimate truth, and is thus free from the pangs of material miseries.

✨ Reflection ✨

As we reflect on the teachings of this chapter, we are reminded of the importance of cultivating a sense of inner peace and detachment from the material world. We are reminded of the importance of controlling our senses, and focusing our minds on the Supreme Lord. We are reminded of the importance of self-realization, and the pursuit of perfection.

May we take these teachings to heart, and may we strive to become the perfect mystics, assured of liberation in the Supreme.
